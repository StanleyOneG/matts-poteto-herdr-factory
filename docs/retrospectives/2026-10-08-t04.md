## Finding

T04 combined an oversized implementation assignment, late native-feasibility checks, an added blocking requirement, repeated evidence errors, and large resumed contexts. Independent review found real defects and corrected requirement overreach. Removing review would not address the main implementation and correction costs.

Compatible Matt and pstack techniques are not inherently in conflict. Running two complete workflow controllers without an explicit composition contract leaves ownership unresolved. Shared behavior-first values and Matt TDD precedence do not decide delegation, design exploration, continuation, or publication authority.

The resulting development policy used one coordinator and one bounded outcome per assignment. An optional `/skill:legion-continue` development router was present locally at that time. It is now archived outside skill discovery, not a required workflow. Normal `/implement` is the next entry for an approved bounded assignment under the current policy.

## What the evidence supports

1. The first writer received all of #5, including implementation, skill loading, approval, children, live trials, and acceptance. After roughly 42 minutes, its report still marked all six criteria incomplete. Later decomposition made the dependent outcomes explicit. The lesson was to assign one verifiable outcome first. #5 was then the acceptance umbrella; the redesign has superseded it.
2. The supervisor required conflict detection across every configured resource root. Native discovery exposed selected winners, not all discarded duplicates. Independent review found that universal hidden-collision requirement absent from the approved spec; the supervisor acknowledged the overreach. The correction also fixed genuine read-proof defects, so its whole cost cannot be attributed to this mistake. Classify each blocking invariant before implementation.
3. Early probes did not prove production startup ordering. A controlled adapter precreated evidence unavailable during native `session_start`. A later correction removed a wait for data produced only after startup returned. Genuine startup eventually succeeded, but no fail-closed post-start provider-request veto was established. This is an unresolved mechanism boundary, not proof that ordinary permission preservation is impossible. Prove required native dependencies early and escalate unsupported guarantees.
4. Setup failures were presented as red evidence, and shell commands that ended with a successful log-tail operation masked failing test exits. An external timeout also cut off a validation batch. Use the existing focused-red helper for its intended purpose. A general exit-preserving runner and CI need separate implementation; prose alone does not supply them.
5. Resumes retained useful knowledge and large histories. All 13 coding/correction runs used Astra despite distinct configured role models. Early hardest-task justification expanded into a default for the whole effort. Keep hard judgments bounded, select models by the configured role, and hand off a current contract rather than repeatedly loading historical briefs.

The final historical gate remained `CHILD_RUNTIME_GATE_BLOCKED`. This retrospective is not a current-code review or a live acceptance result.

## Why TDD precedence was insufficient in the former workflow

The repository's [implement skill](../../.agents/skills/implement/SKILL.md) specifies Matt TDD at agreed seams, regular focused tests and typechecks, a full suite at the end, code review, and a current-branch commit. [matt-tdd](../../.agents/skills/matt-tdd/SKILL.md) supplies behavior-first red/green slices and puts refactoring in review.

The installed poteto-mode at the time added mandatory how and architect triggers, conditional arena, default poteto-agent delegation, principle leaf reads, and audit procedures. Its Feature playbook called for liberal commits, rebasing into ordered commits, and opening a PR. It also carried its own autonomy and continuation rules. Those were workflow decisions, not merely alternative testing advice.

The installed pstack `tdd` skill also permitted skipping an impractical new regression test with an explanation and alternative verification. Under the former spec, Matt's required loop and Legion's approved-exception protocol took precedence over that conditional advice. That spec resolved this testing difference but did not fully resolve the two workflow controllers.

Development therefore needed an explicit owner for scope, design, delegation, review, continuation, and publication. The current policy retains compatible techniques without running both controllers implicitly. It does not rewrite either upstream skill. [#26](https://github.com/StanleyOneG/matts-poteto-herdr-factory/issues/26) formerly tracked product composition and awaited triage; it is now closed as not planned. [#27](https://github.com/StanleyOneG/matts-poteto-herdr-factory/issues/27) remains the separate validation-runner/CI task. The former triage list is not a current direction to expand or resume #5.

## Former product requirements and continuation advice

On 8 October, the former spec required poteto-mode on managed Tribuni, Matt as primary, compatible pstack supplements, and Legatus ownership of the task graph. The bounded Matt-led development workflow did not waive #5's then-required managed-session skill, approval, child-lifecycle, research, and independent-review trials. Changing that product requirement needed explicit approval, not an inference from this retrospective.

The approved redesign has since replaced that contract. Poteto is off by default and only the Emperor enables it; Matt-controlled workflows and poteto are mutually exclusive. Code controls launch, ownership, isolated writes, execution state and authorized effects. Skills, meaningful tests and independent review establish methodology without custom transcript proofs. Ordinary permissions, machine settings and the prohibition on new Legion numeric concurrency/retry/duration/spend caps remain preserved. Current model and native-child boundaries are defined in #1, not by the old managed profile.

Product telemetry was formerly tracked in [#6](https://github.com/StanleyOneG/matts-poteto-herdr-factory/issues/6), and managed compaction in [#8](https://github.com/StanleyOneG/matts-poteto-herdr-factory/issues/8). Both were closed as not planned in the redesign. Those references are historical requirements, not an active telemetry or compaction backlog. Operator-chosen checkpoints still preserve active effects rather than auto-kill work.

The former advice was to reconcile dirty work and actor ownership before resuming #5, separate required provider/model preservation from proposed enforcement mechanisms, and present one bounded outcome for independent review. New work instead follows the replacement outcomes in #1 through normal `/implement` and the current policy. A checkpoint is not issue completion.
